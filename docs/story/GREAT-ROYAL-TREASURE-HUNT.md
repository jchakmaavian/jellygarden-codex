## 1. The promise

**Win a puzzle, make something wonderful happen, and discover what is around the next corner.**

King Leo and Queen Freya are getting their castle ready for the Royal Festival when Mimi the cat discovers a magical map. The map folds into a talkative paper dragon and invites them to find six golden keys. Together, the keys will open a mysterious tower and reveal the kingdom's greatest surprise.

Their adventure takes them through secret castle rooms, a runaway parade, sparkling caves, a friendly royal tournament, and an upside-down invention tower. Every place the player improves helps the journey and becomes part of the final celebration.

Royal advisor Grimm wants to be the first to discover the treasure. His ridiculous shortcuts make him a recurring rival, until his inventions and the friends they have met become essential to finishing the hunt.

**The central question:** What will the six keys unlock?

**The immediate question in each area:** What fun thing will appear when we finish this place?

**Leo's invitation to the player:** “Will you be our Royal Puzzle Partner?”

## 2. The complete story

### Beginning: a map with wings

The castle is busy preparing for the Royal Festival. Alfred is counting banners, Cook is trying to keep Leo away from the cakes, and Freya is planning enough activities for the whole kingdom. Older rooms and unused attractions need attention before the guests arrive.

At the castle gates, Mimi spots a golden ribbon sticking out of a cupboard on a cart of festival decorations. She pulls. A rolled map springs free, loops around Leo's crown, and folds itself into a tiny paper dragon.

His name is Pip. He is the castle's old adventure map, and he has been waiting for someone to open the cupboard.

“Six keys! One enormous surprise! Adventurers, follow me!”

Pip shows six empty key shapes and a drawing of a tower. Leo knows every tower in his castle. He has never seen this one.

Freya suggests following the first clue while they finish the festival preparations. Leo appoints himself Royal Chief Adventurer and invites the player to be their Royal Puzzle Partner. Mimi starts without waiting for a title.

Grimm announces that a proper treasure hunt requires a proper expert. He brings an enormous measuring stick and a very small sandwich.

### Chapter 1: The Castle of Surprises

The first clue takes the team through the castle. Restored rooms reveal hidden inventions: a table that sets itself, a fountain that points with water, and a staircase that plays a tune when someone walks on it.

In the throne room, a hidden viewing door reveals the mystery tower across an enclosed garden. Five small locks ring its door, with a sun-shaped window above them. The team can see their destination, but reaching it will take the whole hunt.

Grimm tries to detect the key with his new treasure machine. It finds spoons, buckles, and eventually his own hat pin. Leo tries solving a musical clue by singing much too loudly. Freya realizes the tune fits a staircase they discovered in an old picture book.

They follow its tune into a secret music room. Leo, Freya, and the player restore the instruments, and Mimi lands on the last piano key. A golden key shaped like a musical note rises from the organ.

Pip's map gains its first key. A little model carousel begins turning inside the mystery tower. The next route appears: the village parade grounds.

### Chapter 2: The Runaway Parade

The village is refitting a magnificent old dragon parade float. The second golden key is hidden inside its clear nose ornament, where it has decorated the float for generations.

When Grimm fits his automatic parade engine, the float starts its route early. It rolls along at walking speed, playing cheerful music and refusing to stop until it reaches the end of its programmed parade. Its wheels automatically pause at unfinished crossings, giving the team time to catch up and prepare the next section.

The adventure becomes a moving celebration. Players rebuild bridges, decorate stalls, arrange a marching band, and help villagers join the procession. Every stop adds another performer or passenger. Grimm's flying carriage, built to overtake the float, settles into a haystack. The villagers help him out, and he helps them build the parade equipment.

Freya discovers the float will stop when it hears the parade's finishing tune. The team gets the band and the final stage ready. At Harvest Court, the music plays, the float takes a bow, and its nose ornament opens to present the Ribbon Key.

Hidden underneath is a tiny train ticket. The next adventure leaves from the old Crystal Express station.

### Chapter 3: The Crystal Express

Leo has always wanted to drive a train. Alfred produces an engineer's cap that is far too large. Mimi immediately falls asleep inside it.

The team restores a little sightseeing railway through sparkling caves. They meet Pebble, a baby dragon who blows warm soap bubbles instead of fire. Pebble has found the third key and uses it as the flag on a homemade toy train.

Pebble happily offers the key when Pip explains the hunt. Freya notices that removing it will leave the little train unfinished. The team decides to build Pebble a proper flag and a wonderful play station on their way through the caves.

Grimm's shortcut machine produces a very long spring instead of a tunnel. Later, he uses the same spring to make a comfortable carriage for Pebble. His invention becomes useful when he listens to what someone actually needs.

At the Crystal Station opening, Pebble puts the new flag on the toy train and gives the Crystal Key to Leo. The real train carries everyone through a glowing tunnel. At the exit, a cheerful invitation awaits: King Haldor challenges them to the Royal Adventure Games. The fourth key is this year's grand prize.

### Chapter 4: The Royal Adventure Games

The northern kingdom is full of bright snow sculptures, colorful flags, and friendly competitors. King Haldor loves games almost as much as he loves announcing them through a very large horn.

Leo's team helps prepare the courses and takes part in a snowball target game, a skating parade, a kite challenge, and a relay of small puzzle stations. Grimm enters with gadgets for every event. Sometimes they work. Sometimes his skis go one way while he remains standing in exactly the same place.

The games reward finishing shared challenges and helping other teams. Haldor explains this at the start. Leo learns to ask Freya for ideas. Freya lets Leo try a funny solution of his own. Grimm gradually discovers that sharing his inventions produces better results than keeping them secret.

In the final relay, a wind-up mascot spins off its route. Leo's team and Haldor's team work together to guide it home. All their earned team pennants complete the finish arch, which opens to reveal the Snowflake Key.

Haldor presents it to the treasure hunters, gives every team a celebration ribbon, and accepts an invitation to Leo's festival. Inside the key's case, the map finds a drawing of an upside-down tower.

### Chapter 5: The Upside-Down Tower

The tower is an old workshop built by the castle's first inventors. Furniture floats on gentle platforms, books rearrange themselves into staircases, and one lift only moves when its passengers hum.

The team follows Pip through the workshop grounds and into increasingly surprising rooms. They discover that the six keys belong to a huge invention called the Wonderworks. Its blueprints connect the castle, village parade, railway, and festival grounds.

The treasure hunt has been guiding them through the places that need to be ready for the Wonderworks to open. Their repairs have already built much of the surprise.

Grimm tries operating the tower alone and strands himself on a gently floating sofa. Freya guides him down. Later, a complicated gear puzzle needs his inventing skills. Leo asks for his help, and Grimm shares his notebook willingly.

In the top workshop, everyone helps bring a giant model of the kingdom to life. It presents the Gear Key. The model shows the last key inside the mystery tower at Leo's castle. Pip unfolds its final instruction: “Bring your keys. Bring your friends.”

### Chapter 6: The Biggest Surprise

The treasure hunters return home. This chapter expands the places the player has already restored into festival attractions. The castle's earlier improvements are still there, and familiar friends arrive to help.

The band brings the parade float. Pebble arrives on the Crystal Express. Haldor brings the games team and a snow-making fan. Cook prepares the feast. Alfred discovers that his neatly organized guest list is now several rooms long.

Grimm tries starting a small preview of the Wonderworks. It produces a giant bubble that carries his hat to the tower balcony. He laughs, asks for help, and shares the controls with Freya. Together, they learn how to start each part in the right order.

Five keys open the outer entrance of the mystery tower. Inside is a final mechanism with stations for music, movement, light, wind, and invention. The friends they have met operate the stations while the player completes the final area tasks. At its center, the sixth key appears: the Sunburst Key.

All six keys fit the main Wonderworks console. The team finishes the viewing gardens and celebration spaces, then gathers in the Grand Courtyard. A golden button rises from the floor.

**PRESS FOR WONDER.**

Leo lifts a finger. Mimi is faster.

The castle unfolds into an enormous festival playground. Tower slides curl into soft landing pools. Fountains fill the sky with rainbow bubbles. A grand carousel rises from the courtyard. The parade float leads the procession, and the little train carries guests between the attractions the player helped create.

Leo thanks the Royal Puzzle Partner. Freya invites everyone inside. Grimm rides on a float shaped like his magnificent hat, proudly waving beside the villagers who helped build it.

Then Pip sneezes out a new scrap of map. It shows a flying island with an enormous pair of footprints.

Leo grins. “Freya... how do you feel about a flying island?”

She opens a picnic basket. “I packed extra sandwiches.”

The Royal Festival story has a complete, happy ending. The flying island is an invitation to a future adventure.

### Character journeys

- **Leo:** “I want to try everything” becomes “Everyone should get a turn.” He remains funny and active throughout.
- **Freya:** Starts by making excellent plans, then learns to leave room for surprising ideas. She makes important discoveries and leads several set pieces herself.
- **Grimm:** Wants his name on the treasure, then discovers the joy of making something other people can use. He contributes before the final chapter so his change feels earned.
- **Pip:** Starts as a guide following an old route and ends as part of a team creating a new adventure.
- **The player:** Starts by opening the festival gates and finishes as the person whose puzzles and improvements made the celebration possible.

## 4. Story rules and game connection

### The six keys

All keys are gold with different, clearly readable shapes. Their shapes match six spaces on Pip's map. Their presentation is distinct from ordinary level stars.

| Key | Shape | Earned at | What the achievement reveals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Melody Key** | Musical note | Area 025 | A model carousel wakes; the village route appears. |
| **Ribbon Key** | Ribbon bow | Area 050 | A model parade float wakes; a train ticket points to the Crystal Express. |
| **Crystal Key** | Crystal cluster | Area 075 | A model train begins moving; Haldor's games invitation arrives. |
| **Snowflake Key** | Snowflake | Area 100 | A model wind wheel turns; the invention tower route appears. |
| **Gear Key** | Round gear | Area 125 | The kingdom model connects the inventions and points home. |
| **Sunburst Key** | Sun with rays | Area 145 | The final Wonderworks control becomes available. |

The first five keys open the mystery tower's outer door at Area 142. The Sunburst Key is earned through the shared challenge inside. All six operate the main console at Area 149; the big reveal occurs at Area 150. The tower's outer entrance and main console are separate mechanisms, so the hunt never requires a key that is locked behind itself.

### How magic works

Pip reveals the next section of the route when an area's final task is completed. He can show pictures, fold into useful shapes, and translate the map's short clues. He cannot lift heavy objects, finish repairs, or create keys.

The castle's old inventions produce playful, local effects when restored. Floating objects follow visible tracks or stay over soft landing places. Their controls are large, readable, and introduced before a character needs them.

The Wonderworks is the connected invention behind those effects. Chapter 1 plants its symbols, Chapter 3 reveals linked parts, and Chapter 5 explains the design. The final transformation pays off things the player has already built.

Keys remain earned. Revisiting or failing a puzzle never removes a key, reverses a completed improvement, or changes a friendship.

### The area loop

1. **Arrive:** One or two short character lines establish a visible goal.
2. **Play:** Complete match-3 levels to earn stars.
3. **Improve:** Spend stars on clear, concrete tasks; show the result immediately.
4. **Discover:** The final task reveals an object, gag, visitor, or clue.
5. **Continue:** Pip's next route or a character's invitation points to the next area.

Ordinary area discoveries use one short dialogue card and a visual. Every fifth area has a milestone scene. Chapter endings provide a larger payoff. Area 145 awards the final key; Area 150 celebrates the complete adventure.

Task scripts below describe narrative order. Star costs and level counts require a separate balance pass when this document is adapted into the game's content format. The proposed first five areas have 4, 5, 5, 5, and 6 tasks. Area 001 now has four one-star tasks on boards 1–4. Its wooden-door artwork, revised objectives, and instant vine clearing have passed player presentation review; see the [development plan](TREASURE-HUNT-DEVELOPMENT-PLAN.md).

### Tone and presentation

- Use bright cartoon color in every location, including caves and older rooms. Start with appealing places that can become more useful, lively, and surprising.
- Give each problem a visible, manageable goal: straighten a track, tune a fountain, finish a float, or invite a band.
- Keep dialogue to one idea per card. Aim for fewer than 18 words per spoken line. Split longer scenes into small, skippable exchanges.
- Put humor in character choices and visual surprises. Grimm gets help after his mishaps and keeps his dignity.
- Introduce friendly competition with its rules explained in advance. Celebrate the other teams as well as Leo's.
- Use festival preparation as anticipation. The festival waits for the player; story dialogue does not impose a countdown.
- Express excitement through movement, discovery, spectacle, and teamwork. The rewritten plot contains no abduction, war, family tragedy, or frightening punishment.
- Let the player see completed work during later visits and the ending. Chapter 6 adds attractions to the restored castle.
- Frame a lost level as another attempt at a puzzle. Characters remain encouraging, and the story waits.

## 5. Screenplay format and opening

**STAGE** describes visible action and audio direction. **NAME:** marks spoken dialogue or a dialogue card. **PLAYER:** marks an actual interaction. **ON SCREEN:** is display text. These labels are authoring instructions, not text shown to players.

Scene IDs refer to story events. A milestone scene plays after the area's final task; it replaces that area's ordinary discovery card. Areas 005 and 025 have single canonical scenes below, referenced wherever needed so they do not play twice.

### OPEN-01 — A ribbon at the gates

**Trigger:** New game. Begin on the first match-3 board, with the festival gates visible behind it. The opening unfolds after the first win and pauses for player taps between dialogue cards.

**STAGE:** Morning sunshine. Bright ivy climbs the towers and stone arch of an already-open entrance. Rolled banners wait on a decoration cart nearby. The first board uses its existing ice-block and score targets; winning earns the star for clearing the vines at the entrance. A hand demonstrates one match.

**PLAYER:** Make the first match; continue to the first win.

**STAGE:** The match effects finish. Show the earned star. Return to the gate area, where Leo and Freya stand beside Alfred's decoration cart. An old cupboard is strapped securely to it. A golden ribbon pokes through its door.

**LEO:** “Welcome, Puzzle Partner!”

**STAGE:** Mimi springs from the cart's step and tugs the ribbon. The cupboard opens. A rolled map pops out, circles Leo's crown, and unfolds into Pip.

**PIP:** “I’m Pip! Six keys lead to one royal surprise!”

**STAGE:** Pip flattens into a map showing six empty key shapes and the mystery tower. Mimi pats the drawing.

**STAGE:** Pip folds back into a dragon. His tail points to the ivy at the gate. Show the first task and the player's available star.

**PLAYER:** Spend the earned star on “Clear the vines.” Continue with Area 001.

**Delivery:** Exactly two post-win dialogue cards, with the map cue on Pip's line. Use the board's visual tutorial without an extra character card. The opening is skippable and can be replayed. If skipped, leave the objective “Prepare the gates for the royal festival!” visible on the area screen.
