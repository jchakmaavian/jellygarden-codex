# Naming research and player journey

Research date: 8 September 2026. Recommendation: **Castle Charm**. Suggested store title: **Castle Charm: Match 3**.

This is a naming and experience-design recommendation based on public store listings, a limited name search, and UX principles. It is not a player study, a forecast of downloads, or confirmation of worldwide name availability. The user requested a castle, king, queen, trailer, and animated storytelling. The proposed cast and magical story remain creative concepts.

## Decision

Choose **Castle Charm** over the earlier **Castlewish** proposal for the current creative direction. Castle gives an immediate setting; Charm can describe both the characters' appeal and the castle's magic. Two familiar words should be straightforward to say and write. These are design judgments to validate with players, not measured advantages.

Use **Castle Charm** on the logo and trailer, and **Castle Charm: Match 3** where the store title needs to communicate gameplay. Keep the tagline **Every match brings magic home.** The title has 21 characters including spaces and punctuation. Apple and Google currently allow up to 30 characters in an app title. [Apple product-page guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/), [Google Play metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en-GB).

The main tradeoff is distinctiveness: Castle is crowded, and Charm can suggest jewelry. The icon, royal pair, living lantern, and actual puzzle imagery must make the game recognizable. Castlewish remains a reasonable alternative if player testing shows stronger recall without confusion.

## Candidate comparison

| Candidate | Strength | Weakness | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Castle Charm** | Familiar wording; castle setting; room for humor, relationships, and magic | Crowded Castle vocabulary; Charm may suggest jewelry | **Recommended balance for this story direction** |
| Castlewish | A distinctive invented compound; strong connection to the Wishlight story | A new word to learn; spoken spacing may vary; needs a gameplay cue | Keep as the strongest alternate |
| Crown Charm | Compact; royal and magical associations | Does not explicitly suggest a castle; could sound like an accessory brand | Weaker setting clarity |
| Castle Story | Immediately describes a narrative setting | An existing castle-renovation puzzle game's title | Reject |

Apple recommends a simple, memorable, easy-to-spell name that indicates the app's purpose and avoids excessive similarity to others. That supports balancing clarity with distinctiveness; it does not settle which candidate players will prefer. [Apple product-page guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/).

Direct public Google Play searches in US English displayed 12 results each for Castle Charm and Castlewish. Neither visible set contained an exact title match. Public listing comparisons included US App Store pages and English Google Play pages with US or UK locale settings. No exhaustive country, language, domain, or trademark check was performed. No name was registered. No exact Castle Charm title surfaced in the sampled searches; worldwide availability remains unconfirmed.

## Proposed player journey

This is a hypothesis map, not a map derived from interviews or analytics. The initial audience assumption is a casual puzzle player looking for a satisfying short break, light story, and visible progress. It makes no assumptions about gender or age.

| Stage | Player's question and emotional need | Experience we should design | What to observe in a prototype |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Notice the game | Is this for me? Curiosity and familiarity | Castle Charm appears with the king, queen, Pip, and a clear glimpse of the puzzle board | After five seconds, can someone describe the expected game and tone? |
| Inspect the store page | What will I actually do? Clarity and trust | Match 3 in the title; screenshots explain puzzles, castle changes, and character moments | Can the player explain the main activity without mistaking a cinematic for gameplay? |
| Play the first minute | Can I do this? Confidence and control | A brief, skippable introduction, a readable goal, and one contextual swap prompt | Can the player make the first move and explain its result without extra help? |
| Earn the first reward | Did my action matter? Competence and ownership | A visible castle improvement, a small decoration choice, and a short royal reaction | Can the player connect the puzzle reward to the castle change? |
| Struggle or lose | What happened, and can I improve? Understanding and agency | Clear remaining goals, understandable outcomes, and a straightforward retry | Can the player explain the loss and identify a useful next attempt? |
| Return and share | What happens next, and what was it called? Curiosity and recall | A visible next chapter goal, optional recap, and consistent name/icon across touchpoints | Can the player recall the name and next goal after a delay? |

Recognition generally requires less memory effort than unaided recall. Apply this by keeping the title, icon, character silhouettes, and story symbols consistent and making immediate actions visible. It does not prove that Castle Charm is more memorable than Castlewish. [Nielsen Norman Group: recognition and recall](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/).

## Story and cinematic implications

Keep the proposed king-and-queen partnership and Wishlight incident from [the creative treatment](NAME-STORY-AND-CINEMATICS.md). The castle's charm can be expressed through rooms with personalities, warm character comedy, and magical restoration. Wishlights remain an internal story concept; they need not also be the brand name.

The trailer should connect a funny royal problem to actual match-3 play and a visible castle payoff. Opening story scenes should be skippable and lead promptly into play. Use the same characters, puzzle pieces, and VFX across the trailer and game. These are planned experiences, not existing finished assets.

Suggested store copy for later creative testing:

- Title: **Castle Charm: Match 3**
- Apple subtitle: **A king, a queen, your castle**
- Short description: **Match colorful puzzles, restore a magical castle, and meet its king and queen.**

This is positioning work; mobile exporting and submission remain deferred.

## Validate the decision before a broad rebrand

Run a small qualitative pilot with 8–12 relevant players using Castle Charm, Castlewish, and Crown Charm. Hold artwork and gameplay description constant and vary presentation order. Show each title briefly, ask what game they expect, and check spoken pronunciation and written recall after a delay. Then show the story premise and ask which title fits and why.

Look for recurring confusion, misspellings, mistaken genre expectations, and difficulty distinguishing other titles. Do not treat such a small pilot as a statistically reliable conversion test. If Castlewish is remembered more accurately while communicating the premise just as clearly, reconsider the recommendation.

Current outcome: use **Castle Charm** for the next creative exploration. The Unity folder, GitHub repository, application identifier, and save identity are unchanged. Gameplay and animation production have not been modified by this research pass.
